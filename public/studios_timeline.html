LEARNING IN ACTION

Active Studios: Grade 3

A year-long journey guided by real-world expert missions, not subject divisions.

ACTIVE STUDIO (LONG-YEAR)

Our Living Campus

Instead of separate ecosystems, weather, physics, or geometry units, Grade 3 learners spend the year investigating a single living system on their campus. There are no traditional lesson announcements. Instead, the journey is driven by video messages from real-world guides, biologists, engineers, and community stakeholders.

1
PHASE 1: THE INITIAL MISSION

Apex Parks Department

🌳
▶ Play Video Message
"Hello everyone. We need your help. We've noticed that some trees around our parks are dying, while others are growing really well. We don't understand why. Could your Studio investigate and help us?"
Student Agency

The mission begins. No teacher stands at the board saying, "Today we are learning ecosystems." Instead, the learner's mindset is: "We need to help Apex Parks."

2
PHASE 2: ONE MONTH LATER

Park Manager Update

📋
▶ Play Video Message
"Thank you for your first report. One thing surprised us... The healthy trees all seem to be on one side of the park."
Spatial Sensemaking

A new mystery emerges. Learners investigate solar mapping, micro-climates, and soil differences to explain this geographic pattern.

3
PHASE 3: METEOROLOGIST ALERT

Meteorologist Sarah Miller

⚡
▶ Play Video Message
"A storm is coming next week. Do you think the trees will be affected? Let's check the local wind vectors and soil saturation models."
Earth Systems

Weather dynamics are introduced. No "weather unit" is announced. Learners study air pressure, wind forces, and precipitation to protect their study trees.

4
PHASE 4: BIOLOGIST INQUIRY

Dr. Marcus Vance, Biologist

🔬
▶ Play Video Message
"Hi Studio Team. We found a new insect living only on some of the distressed trees in the park. Can you help us catalog its feeding patterns?"
Life Science

Ecosystem interactions come alive. Students investigate symbiotic relationships, parasites, and food webs to identify if the insect is a cause or a symptom.

5
PHASE 5: DESIGN CHALLENGE

Lead Engineer Clara Diaz

📐
▶ Play Video Message
"Could you design something that protects these young trees from high-velocity wind loads during the storm season?"
Engineering & Design

Physical science and design processes merge. Students build, test, and optimize models of physical tree-bracing structures using everyday materials.

6
PHASE 6: DATA MODELING

Mathematician Liam Kincaid

📊
▶ Play Video Message
"Can you compare the growth rates and mechanical trunk measurements we've recorded over the last four weeks? Let's check for correlations."
Mathematics

Quantitative analysis is applied. Mathematics becomes the language to prove or disprove hypotheses about soil pH and tree growth rates.

7
PHASE 7: ADVOCACY & CIVICS

City Council Coordinator

🏛️
▶ Play Video Message
"Could you prepare a report for the upcoming City Council session? We need to vote on whether to fund the soil treatment plan."
Literacy & ELA

English Language Arts is integrated. Students write evidence-based arguments and practice public presentation skills to advocate for their local ecosystem.

8
PHASE 8: DEEPENING INQUIRY (AI EXPERT)

Dr. Maya Chen, Forest Ecologist

🎓
▶ Play Video Message
"Hi Studio Team. I'm a researcher at UNC. We heard you've been studying our trees and doing excellent work. I have another question about root networks..."
Systems Thinking

AI-generated video messages from global experts check in to prompt higher-order questions, validating the students' identity as active, capable scientific investigators.
